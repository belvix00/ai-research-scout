AI Research Scout Resources — 2026-05-11

Core DL/LLM research

arXiv — Long Context Pre-Training with Lighthouse Attention

Type: Paper

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06554v1

Summary: A training-only hierarchical attention wrapper that compresses/decompresses long sequences around ordinary SDPA, then removes the wrapper near the end of training to recover a full-attention model. Collected because it treats long-context pretraining as an optimization and curriculum problem, not only a serving problem. The mechanism to watch is whether gradient-free symmetric Q/K/V pooling preserves enough training signal before final full-attention recovery.

Personalization: medium

arXiv — The Structural Origin of Attention Sink: Variance Discrepancy, Super Neurons, and Dimension Disparity

Type: Paper

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06611v1

Summary: A mechanistic account of attention sinks that traces them to value aggregation, variance discrepancy, super-neuron amplification, and dimension disparity in first-token representations. It includes causal interventions that recreate sinks at arbitrary positions and proposes head-wise RMSNorm as a control. Collected because it turns a common long-context/inference pathology into a testable architectural mechanism.

Personalization: medium

arXiv — Continuous Latent Diffusion Language Model

Type: Paper

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06548v1

Summary: Cola DLM separates text generation into text-to-latent mapping, continuous latent diffusion over global semantics, and conditional decoding. It is useful because it asks whether language modeling can move some reasoning/planning burden from left-to-right token prediction into a continuous semantic prior. The mechanism signal is global plan transport versus local surface realization.

Personalization: medium

arXiv — Efficient Pre-Training with Token Superposition

Type: Paper

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06546v1

Summary: Token-Superposition Training combines many contiguous tokens into one multi-hot training target during an efficient phase, then recovers normal autoregressive training. The reported claim is up to 2.5x pretraining time reduction under equal-loss settings at larger scale. Collected because it reframes early pretraining as a deliberately lossy throughput phase followed by recovery.

Personalization: low

arXiv — EMO: Pretraining Mixture of Experts for Emergent Modularity

Type: Paper

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06663v1

Summary: EMO encourages tokens from the same document to choose from a shared expert pool so document-level domains induce coherent expert groupings without hand-written priors. This continues the week’s MoE-capacity thread but with a different mechanism: document boundaries as a weak modularity signal. The key experiment is whether expert subsets become independently usable rather than merely sparse.

Personalization: medium

Training/RL/math/mech interp

arXiv — Can RL Teach Long-Horizon Reasoning to LLMs? Expressiveness Is Key

Type: Paper

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06638v1

Summary: ScaleLogic provides controlled logical reasoning environments with independent axes for proof depth and logical expressiveness. The paper reports power-law RL training compute versus reasoning depth, with the exponent increasing as the logic becomes more expressive. Collected because it makes “reasoning difficulty” decomposable and measurable rather than a single benchmark score.

Personalization: medium

arXiv — Recursive Agent Optimization

Type: Paper

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06639v1

Summary: RAO trains agents that can recursively spawn/delegate sub-tasks to copies of themselves, making recursive inference-time scaling part of the policy. This is a concrete agent-RL lifecycle mechanism: delegation, communication, subproblem boundaries, and wall-clock parallelism become trainable choices. It is a good candidate for toy-environment reproduction because the intervention is structural.

Personalization: medium

arXiv — Positive-Only Policy Optimization with Implicit Negative Gradients

Type: Paper

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06650v1

Summary: POPO asks whether RLVR can learn from online positive rollouts only, using bounded importance sampling over positives so implicit negative gradients arise from probability redistribution. It is a useful counterpoint to GRPO-style grouped positive/negative rollout framing. The mechanism signal is whether sparse binary rewards need sampled failures, or whether reinforcing positives can provide enough contrast.

Personalization: medium

arXiv — Optimizer-Model Consistency: Full Finetuning with the Same Optimizer as Pretraining Forgets Less

Type: Paper

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06654v1

Summary: This paper argues that the pretraining optimizer shapes activation regularization and local landscape structure, so SFT should use a compatible optimizer to reduce forgetting. It compares optimizer choices including Muon and AdamW and reports different forgetting/reasoning tradeoffs. Collected because optimizer identity becomes part of the model state, not an interchangeable training detail.

Personalization: low

arXiv — SoftSAE: Dynamic Top-K Selection for Adaptive Sparse Autoencoders

Type: Mech interp

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06610v1

Summary: SoftSAE replaces fixed TopK sparsity with a differentiable dynamic Top-K operator so each input can choose a sparsity level matching local intrinsic complexity. Collected because fixed-k SAEs may underfit complex examples and add noisy features to simple examples. The mechanism question is whether adaptive explanation capacity improves causal intervention prediction, not just reconstruction.

Personalization: medium

arXiv — Patch-Effect Graph Kernels for LLM Interpretability

Type: Mech interp

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06480v1

Summary: Activation-patching profiles are converted into patch-effect graphs over model components, then compared with graph kernels. The paper tests this on IOI and related GPT-2 Small tasks and finds local edge-slot features preserve discriminative circuit structure. Collected because it proposes a data structure for comparing causal-patching results across prompts/tasks instead of hand-inspecting heatmaps.

Personalization: low

OpenReview — Decomposing LLM Computation with Jets

Type: Math/theory

Link: https://openreview.net/forum?id=u6JLh0BO5h

Summary: Jet expansions are proposed as operators for decomposing LLM computation into explicit input-output paths plus remainders, generalizing truncated Taylor-style analysis. This is useful as a possible formal language for cutting entangled computations into inspectable pieces. It is high-level, but worth tracking alongside graph-kernel and SAE approaches to mechanistic decomposition.

Personalization: low

Audio/TTS/speech

arXiv — PairAlign: Sequence Tokenization via Self-Alignment with Applications to Audio Tokenization

Type: Audio/TTS

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06582v1

Summary: PairAlign treats audio tokenization as conditional sequence generation, training two content-preserving views to predict each other’s token sequences while unrelated sequences act as competitors. This directly targets sequence consistency, compactness, EOS/length control, and edit-similarity preservation rather than only local codec reconstruction. Collected because audio tokens are increasingly the interface between speech perception, memory, and generation.

Personalization: medium

arXiv — TextPro-SLM: Your Speech LLM Can Be a Prosody-Aware Text LLM

Type: Audio/TTS

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05927v1

Summary: TextPro-SLM reduces speech/text modality gap from the input side by pairing synchronized text tokens with prosody embeddings before the LLM. It claims strong paralinguistic understanding with only roughly 1,000 hours of LLM training audio. The mechanism is factorized input representation: text-like semantic tokens plus prosody channels instead of forcing all speech information through one latent.

Personalization: medium

arXiv — Task-Aware Answer Preservation under Audio Compression for Large Audio Language Models

Type: Audio/TTS

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06631v1

Summary: This work evaluates audio compression by excess answer-error in query families, especially worst-affected families, rather than by aggregate audio quality. It proposes a statistical sign-off protocol for safe compression budgets. Collected because it exposes the same aggregate-metric camouflage problem in LALMs: compression can look fine overall while destroying one class of downstream answers.

Personalization: medium

arXiv — JASTIN: Aligning LLMs for Zero-Shot Audio and Speech Evaluation via Natural Language Instructions

Type: Audio/TTS

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04505v1

Summary: JASTIN connects a frozen audio encoder to an LLM through an audio adapter and trains instruction-style evaluation across speech, sound, and music. It is a signal that audio evaluation is moving toward flexible language-conditioned judging rather than fixed objective metrics. The mechanism to scrutinize is calibration and domain generalization of the adapter, not only correlation with human ratings.

Personalization: low

Type: Infra/GPU

Link: https://www.together.ai/blog/serving-deepseek-v4-why-million-token-context-is-an-inference-systems-problem

PyTorch Blog — SMG: The Case for Disaggregating CPU from GPU in LLM Serving

Type: Infra/GPU

Link: https://pytorch.org/blog/lightseek-smg/

Summary: A production serving writeup arguing for separating CPU orchestration from GPU execution paths to avoid bottlenecks such as Python/GIL pressure at scale. It is relevant because scheduler/orchestrator design can determine whether model-side optimizations are visible in production throughput. The mechanism signal is CPU-GPU boundary placement in serving systems.

Personalization: low

arXiv — Litespark Inference on Consumer CPUs: Custom SIMD Kernels for Ternary Neural Networks

Type: Infra/GPU

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06485v1

Summary: Litespark targets ternary-weight LLMs with custom SIMD kernels that replace dense floating-point matmuls with integer add/subtract style operations. Reported speedups on Apple Silicon, Intel, and AMD suggest cheap local inference paths for specialized low-bit models. Collected because ternary inference is not just quantization; it needs matching kernels to realize the theoretical compute win.

Skeptical or failure signal

arXiv — When Audio-Language Models Fail to Leverage Multimodal Context for Dysarthric Speech Recognition

Type: Audio/TTS

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02782v1

Summary: A benchmark on dysarthric ASR finds that current audio-language models do not meaningfully use diagnosis labels, clinician ratings, or richer clinical descriptions at inference time; prompting can even degrade WER. LoRA context-dependent adaptation helps much more. Collected as a failure signal: models may retrieve or receive context without actually conditioning their acoustic decisions on it.
