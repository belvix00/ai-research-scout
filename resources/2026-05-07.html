AI Research Scout Resources — 2026-05-07

Bucket: Core DL/LLM research

arXiv — Transformers with Selective Access to Early Representations

Type: Paper

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03953v1

Summary: This paper tests a transformer variant where later layers can selectively reuse representations from the earliest layers. It is worth collecting because it treats depth not as a one-way compression stack, but as a set of information surfaces that later computation may need to recover. The mechanism signal is early-feature preservation: low-level or lexical features may be lost through repeated residual transformations unless the architecture exposes a controlled access path.

Personalization: medium

arXiv/Hugging Face — OpenSeeker-v2: Pushing the Limits of Search Agents with Informative and High-Difficulty Trajectories

Type: Paper

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04036v1

Summary: OpenSeeker-v2 claims strong search-agent results from only 10.6k SFT trajectories, using harder and more informative synthesized traces rather than a full CPT+SFT+RL industrial recipe. The useful mechanism is data curriculum design for agentic search: larger knowledge-graph exploration, broader tools, and low-step filtering create trajectories dense enough to imitate. It is a good counterweight to assuming every frontier search-agent improvement requires RL.

Personalization: medium

arXiv — Rethinking Reasoning-Intensive Retrieval: Evaluating and Advancing Retrievers in Agentic Search Systems

Type: Paper

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04018v1

Summary: This work focuses on retrieval for downstream reasoning instead of topical similarity alone. It matters because agentic search quality often fails before generation: the model receives plausible but non-complementary evidence. The mechanism is evidence diversity/complementarity under multi-step reasoning, a different retrieval objective than standard nearest-neighbor semantic match.

Personalization: low

arXiv — Two Calls, Two Moments, and the Vote-Accuracy Curve of Repeated LLM Inference

Type: Paper

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03379v1

Summary: This paper analyzes repeated sampling/test-time compute through the latent distribution of correctness across examples, not only one-call accuracy. It is useful because repeated inference gains depend on whether errors are independent, correlated, or concentrated in hard items. The mechanism signal is a vote-accuracy curve: when extra calls help, saturate, or amplify correlated wrong answers.

Personalization: medium

Type: Paper

Link: https://huggingface.co/papers/2605.02913

Summary: This survey decomposes RL rollouts for reasoning LLMs into Generate, Filter, Control, and Replay stages. It is valuable because it moves attention from optimizer branding to the trajectory lifecycle that creates training signal. The mechanism is rollout design as the real data-generating process for post-training: candidate topology, verifier gating, budget control, and artifact reuse.
